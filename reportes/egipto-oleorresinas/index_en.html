Oleoresins in Egypt

Egypt imports its spice extracts from India and China and exports mainly to Saudi Arabia, Libya and France. It is a small market (US$6.3 M imported in 2025) and constrained by the exchange rate. As an origin, Egypt matters for seed spices, not for black pepper, turmeric or paprika oleoresins.

Three takeaways for the commercial team:

  • A small, concentrated import market. Two origins explain 91% of value in 2025, and their weights swing every year: India held 75% in 2022, 47% in 2023, 60% in 2024 and 81% in 2025. China went from 12% to 47%, then to 30% and 10%.
  • The brakes are the dollar, the paperwork and freight. Foreign-currency availability, the advance cargo registration (ACID) and the rise in freight from India since the Hormuz crisis.
  • As an origin, Egypt is a seed-spice source. Cumin, coriander, fennel, caraway, anise and fenugreek. Its oleoresins and oils are a niche business with few verifiable players.

Two warnings about the data

  • The reported weight looks estimated. In each year every partner shows the same value per kilo (≈US$23.5 in 2024 for India, China and Indonesia). That happens when weight is derived from value. Egypt reports only at 6 digits, with no national lines separating oleoresins. We do not use it as a price.
  • Neighbouring heading: vegetable extracts HS 1302.19 totalled US$6.4 M in 2024 (US$8.0 M in 2022). Suppliers are Spain (24%), China (22%), Denmark (16%) and Türkiye (10%): a different botanical-extract market with a more European origin.

Trend reading

  • Paprika and capsicum: firm to rising from India (spot up 10–15% year on year in early 2026). Chinese colour is stable or softer on more pepper-for-pigment acreage.
  • Turmeric: sharp, recent rise in the raw material. It should reach the oleoresin with a one to three month lag: expect higher quotes in the first quarter of 2027.
  • Black pepper: firm and high at origin.
  • Cumin: Egyptian is falling while Indian is rising. A cumin oleoresin made from Egyptian seed gains competitiveness.

Exchange rate

The dollar quoted between 53.36 and 53.84 EGP at Egyptian banks from 12 to 15 November 2026. A 2024 assessment of the Egyptian market describes a sharp fall in imports because of foreign-currency shortages. We found no official Central Bank of Egypt figure for November.

Freight from India

Customers report much higher freight. Public data supports this: the Hormuz crisis, from 28 February 2026, pushed up freight rates and war and fuel surcharges. Since October carriers are returning to Suez and rates are easing, but they remain above the pre-crisis level.

What it means for oleoresin

  • In a full container it weighs little. With a US$5,000 surcharge and 20 t of cargo, the cost is about US$0.25/kg (illustrative calculation). Against US$25–40/kg for a 40,000 CU paprika, that is under 1% of value.
  • In small lots it weighs far more. For consolidated cargo (LCL), samples and orders of one or two drums, freight and surcharges per kilo are several times higher. This is where customers feel it.
  • The route does not cross Hormuz. India–Alexandria heads for the Red Sea and Suez. The effect arrives indirectly: general surcharges, reassigned vessels, war-risk insurance and costlier fuel.
  • The risk is lead time and space. Diversions and congestion lengthen transit, and Egypt requires an ACID before shipping. Plan ahead and ask for prices with an explicit incoterm (FOB or CIF) and a validity date for surcharges.
  • Direction: an 18 November report says carriers may secure only part of the November increases they announced. An escalation in Hormuz is the main upside risk.

Sources: Drewry and Xeneta via Sourcing Journal, Cyprus Shipping News (18 Nov 2026) and SAFETY4SEA; Maersk 2026 notices (India–Mediterranean ECS and Egypt THC); Hellenic Shipping News. Request a firm quotation from Maersk, MSC or Hapag-Lloyd from Nhava Sheva or Mundra to Alexandria or Port Said.

Origins supplying Egypt

India and China hold 91% of value (2025). The supplier names below come from global directories, and are not verified against shipments to Egypt:

  • India: Plant Lipids, Synthite, Akay (Oterra), Mane Kancor, Vidya Herbs.
  • China: Chenguang Biotech (paprika and capsicum), Henan Sunlight.
  • Other partners 2022–2024: Sri Lanka (5% in 2022), Indonesia (4% in 2024), United Kingdom (3% in 2024), Lebanon.

Historical Zauba records (2013–2016) show India shipping seed oleoresins to Egypt (coriander, cumin, celery, fenugreek, fennel), which suggests Egypt imported them rather than produced them. We found no Egyptian producer of paprika, capsicum, pepper or turmeric oleoresin with evidence. In seed oils, A. Fakhry, Fridal and Al Sheikh appear, with no capacity data. Some Plant Lipids and Akay listings appear on B2B platforms as "supplied from Egypt". They are likely intermediaries; we do not treat them as Egyptian production.

Regulation and logistics

  • ACID (Advance Cargo Information): mandatory for sea cargo since October 2021 and for air cargo since 1 January 2026. The importer requests it on the Nafeza platform using the proforma invoice. Without an ACID the cargo cannot enter. It must appear on the commercial invoice, certificate of origin and transport documents. Valid for six months.
  • Sanitary requirements (per a summary of the USDA 2025 FAIRS report, not opened): the positive list of colours and additives is NFSA Decision 4/2020. The certificate of analysis must state scientific name and concentration. If the shipment declares halal, the certificate is issued by IS EG Halal. Import VAT is 14%.
  • Foreign currency: access to dollars has constrained ingredient imports. Agree payment terms and lead times with each customer.
  • Not verified: duty and sanitary registration applicable to HS 3301.90 in Egypt. Pending a query to the Egyptian Customs Authority.

Reading for Robertet

Opportunity

  • Serve houses with a local presence (Symrise, Givaudan, Kerry) with oleoresins of stable specification, not on price.
  • Source Egyptian seeds (cumin, coriander, fennel, caraway, fenugreek) for in-house essential oils and extracts.

Risk

  • Dollar pricing against a market that switches between India and China every year.
  • Payments and foreign currency. The local importer carries the dollar-access risk.

Pending

  • Mirror data from India (reporter 699, partner Egypt) by national line: it would split out the oleoresin share of 60–81% of Egypt's imports.
  • Company-level customs data, and confirming who buys oleoresin among the P1 and P2 houses.
  • HS 3301.90 duty.
  • Recent FOB quotes from India for paprika, pepper and turmeric.

So what

Egypt is a niche account, not a volume market. In the project tender (black pepper, turmeric, nutmeg, clove, celery) none of the main references is Egyptian in origin. The value for Robertet sits on two fronts: serving the houses with a local presence and sourcing seeds and oils from Egypt.
